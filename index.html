Why it stays blocked

The value is clear. The path to it isn't.

Strong teams, useful data, clear potential value, and no acceptable way to analyze it together.

  1. Legal reviewA data request turns into a negotiation over IP and usage rights.
  2. Data requestsPartners ask for raw data that the other side cannot release.
  3. SanitizationAnonymizing until it's safe often removes the signal you needed.
  4. Sequential back-and-forthEach side analyzes its half, then waits on the other. Weeks turn into months, or the question gets dropped.
A different model

Approve the analysis, not the data transfer.

Partners agree on what runs and what each one receives. No partner receives another partner's raw data.

1Define

Agree on the question

The question, the parties, the inputs each one contributes, and the output each one needs.

2Approve

Each owner approves

Each data owner approves the analysis, its inputs, and which results go to whom.

3Analyze

Run it in isolation

The analysis runs in an isolated environment, designed so no partner sees another's inputs.

4Release

Governed insight per partner

Each participant receives only the result it is eligible to see. Every step is logged.

Collaboration is not visibility

What each partner sees, and never sees.

Confidentiality can fail in two common ways: through access to raw data, and through results that reveal too much. Multyx is designed to govern both.

Each partner sees

  • Its own data, under its own control
  • The analysis it approved, before it runs
  • Who is in the collaboration, and the agreed question
  • The specific result it is eligible to act on

No partner sees

  • Another partner's raw data
  • The full combined analysis output
  • Results meant for someone else
  • Results from analyses it did not approve
